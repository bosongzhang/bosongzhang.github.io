Enhanced Efficacy Amplifies Tropical Cyclones Intensification in Warmer Climates

Warming strengthens high-intensity tropical cyclone tails through combined increases in potential intensity and storm efficacy.

Overview: In a high-resolution TC-permitting framework, warming increases tropical cyclone intensity through two coupled pathways: higher potential intensity and higher efficacy in approaching that limit. This combined PI-efficacy amplification explains stronger growth of Category 3-5 frequencies than PI-only scaling. Decomposition analyses further show that ventilation changes alone do not account for the efficacy increase, indicating a distinct thermodynamic-dynamic pathway that must be included in future intensity projections.

Scientific Logic

  • Question: Why does tropical cyclone intensification increase more than expected from PI changes under warming?
  • Method: TC-permitting control versus +2K experiments with decomposition of intensity-distribution changes into PI and efficacy contributions.
  • Mechanism: Environmental warming raises both PI and the realized storm efficiency in approaching PI; ventilation changes alone cannot explain this efficacy increase.
  • Main Findings: Increases in Category 3-5 frequency are amplified by efficacy gains, so PI-only scaling underestimates future high-intensity tail changes.

Key Findings

  • Warming increases both PI and realized efficacy, and their combined effect amplifies the high-intensity tail.
  • PI-only projections understate Category 3-5 increases relative to decomposition-based estimates.
  • Efficacy gains persist after accounting for ventilation-related changes, implying additional controlling physics.
